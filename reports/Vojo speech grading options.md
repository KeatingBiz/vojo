# Measure speech before training a coach

Vojo’s daily grade already does the part of speech coaching that software can count, and a custom model trained on real coaches will not make those counts more true. A finished answer of up to 60 seconds is recorded as mono AAC, resampled to 16 kHz, transcribed by Deepgram Nova-3 or AssemblyAI Universal-2 with fillers explicitly kept, then scored by local math: a closed filler lexicon, words per minute, pauses, Praat pitch spread, and an uptalk slope. Claude Haiku writes the content judgment and the coaching sentences, and it is forbidden from inventing confidence. The APIs that actually track filler words are a short list with an opt-in flag; the APIs that advertise “tone” are mostly scoring the transcript, not the voice. The cheaper path that looks attractive, Groq’s Whisper Turbo at **$0.04 per hour**, is the one that deletes the tokens the score is built on: Whisper Large v3’s English filler F1 on a 2026 verbatim benchmark is **9.4%**. The cheaper path that also raises quality is inside the current stack: bill Deepgram as the pre-recorded call the server already makes (**$0.0043 per minute**, against the **$0.0077** streaming rate written in `config.py`), switch Praat from raw to filtered autocorrelation, and score loudness and voice quality that the code already almost computes. A from-scratch articulation coach cannot be incredibly accurate. Published machine–human correlations sit near how much raters agree with each other, about **r = 0.6** on spontaneous delivery and **r = 0.78** when the construct is delivery features, and expert agreement on where a pronunciation error sits is only **kappa ≈ 0.49**.

## The daily score is four curves on one phone clip

Every daily prompt gives the speaker 30 seconds to think and 60 seconds to talk. The recorder writes mono AAC at 24 kHz and 48 kbps, posts `clip.m4a` plus the prompt text to `POST /analyze`, and the server runs ffmpeg to 16 kHz mono PCM, rejecting clips under **5 seconds**, over **180 seconds**, or quieter than a mean RMS of **0.004**. `measure()` then transcribes, runs a first pass over ambiguous fillers, and computes pacing and prosody before one Claude call. The number the user sees is a weighted sum of unrounded sub-scores: **fillers 0.30, pacing 0.25, prosody 0.25, content 0.20**, with the first three renormalized if the model never returns content. Pacing itself is **0.6 × WPM + 0.4 × pauses**. Prosody, which the card calls delivery, is **0.6 × pitch variation + 0.4 × uptalk**. Content is **0.45 on-topic + 0.35 structure + 0.20 clarity**, with no numeric anchors in the prompt.

Two speech APIs are implemented, and exactly one runs. Deepgram is preferred when `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY` is set: `POST https://api.deepgram.com/v1/listen` with `model=nova-3`, `filler_words=true`, `punctuate=true`, and `smart_format=true`. AssemblyAI is the fallback: upload, then `speech_models: ["universal-2"]` with `disfluencies: true`. Neither request asks for diarization, sentiment, or emotion. Pitch is local Praat through `praat-parselmouth`, floor **75 Hz**, ceiling **500 Hz**, frame step **10 ms**. The Haiku call is `claude-haiku-5-5` with thinking disabled, max 2048 tokens, and a schema that can confirm ambiguous filler indices the first pass already flagged. It cannot add a filler the lexicon missed, and `scores.confidence` is not in that schema. Confidence is the equal average of four local curves: filler rate, pause score, pitch-variation score, and uptalk score. Word-level ASR confidence is stored and never read, so a shaky “um” counts the same as a certain one.

The thresholds that turn those measurements into points are product choices, not published coaching standards. Fillers score 100 at or below **1 per minute** and 0 at **12 per minute**. WPM scores 100 inside **130–170**, and the flags fire above **190** or below **110**, using every word including fillers divided by wall-clock duration, so silence lowers the rate. Pauses are gaps of at least **300 ms** between words; the config comment calls shorter gaps “articulation, not pauses,” and then throws them away. Pitch variation is the standard deviation of semitones relative to the clip’s own median: monotone is flagged below **1.5 semitones**, the score hits 100 at **2.5**, and it hits 0 at **0.3**, so a clip can be labeled monotone and still receive a mid score. Uptalk is a least-squares slope on the last **0.40 seconds** of each punctuation-delimited statement, rising if the slope exceeds **4 semitones per second**. Questions are dropped. If fewer than three statements can be classified, the uptalk term is a flat **85**, which still props up the confidence chip. Energy (20 ms RMS and its 10th and 90th percentiles), pitch drift over the first and last 15 seconds, and 10-second WPM windows are computed and then kept out of the score. The grader has no score for articulation, pronunciation, resonance, breath, accent, emotion category, or a second speaker. A whisper and a shout that clear the silence gate can share a delivery score. The loading line “Reading your tone...” is ahead of the code: tone, as implemented, is pitch spread, uptalk, and two unused fields.

## Filler-preserving APIs exist, and the cheap ones delete um

Preserving “um” and “uh” is a flag, not a default, at the two vendors Vojo already calls. Deepgram’s `filler_words` defaults to false, and when it is false those tokens are stripped. When it is true, Nova models emit only **uh, um, mhmm, mm-mm, uh-uh, uh-huh, and nuh-uh**, with no duration cutoff, and the list does not include “like,” “so,” or “right” ([Deepgram filler words](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/filler-words)). AssemblyAI removes fillers unless `disfluencies` is true. Both the filler doc and the models reference agree that this flag works on **Universal-2**, which is the model the server sends. They contradict each other on Universal-3.5 Pro: the filler page says the flag is supported, and the models reference says the parameter is silently ignored and that prompting (“tag filler words like um and uh”) is the path that works ([AssemblyAI filler words](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/pre-recorded-audio/include-filler-words); [AssemblyAI models reference](https://www.assemblyai.com/llms/models.md)). Speechmatics tags hesitations by default, including lengthened spellings, and removes them only if asked; “like,” “so,” and “right” are still ordinary words ([Speechmatics formatting](https://docs.speechmatics.com/speech-to-text/formatting)). Gemini’s verbatim mode is documented as keeping fillers and is required if word timestamps are on; smart formatting, which strips them, cannot be combined with timestamps ([Gemini transcription guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/transcribe?hl=zh-tw)). No fetched speech-to-text doc treats discourse markers as disfluencies. Haiku’s second job, confirming ambiguous “like,” “so,” and “right,” is work the transcription vendors do not do. That split matches the old NIST approach: more than 95% of filled pauses were “uh, um, eh, ah,” and those words were filled pauses more than 95% of the time, while “you know,” “like,” and “so” needed context ([Lease, Johnson, and Charniak, RT-04](https://mattlease.com/papers/rt04-paper.pdf)).

The 2026 comparison that scores fillers separately, rather than rewarding a cleaned transcript, is the Nyra verbatim benchmark (English n = 4,957). Whisper Large v3, the family Groq serves, posts filler F1 **9.4%** and verbatim WER **10.2%**. Deepgram Nova-3 posts filler F1 **45.7%**, disfluency F1 **57.3%**, and verbatim WER **6.3%**. The README does not record the query string, so that row is not evidence about the `filler_words=true` setting Vojo sends. AssemblyAI Universal-3 Pro with an explicit prompt posts filler F1 **90.8%**, verbatim WER **4.5%**, and cutoff F1 **27.9%**. ElevenLabs Scribe v2 and Microsoft MAI-Transcribe-1.5 lead the commercial filler rows at **95.5%** and **95.8%**, and MAI’s vocal-sound F1 is **0** ([Nyra Verbatim Speech Benchmark](https://github.com/nyrahealth/nyra_verbatim_speech_benchmark)). A lower general WER earned by deleting “um” is a regression for this product. Groq’s own docs advertise **10.3%** WER for `whisper-large-v3` and **12%** for turbo, with no disfluency protocol, at **$0.111** and **$0.04** per hour, and a 10-second minimum that does not bind on a one-minute answer ([Groq speech-to-text](https://console.groq.com/docs/speech-to-text); [Groq pricing](https://home.cloud.groq.io/pricing)). On a second study, Whisper still transcribed only **8.7%** of Switchboard filled pauses ([FluencyBank Timestamped](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12379651/)).

The dollar error in the repo points the other way. `config.py` logs Deepgram at **$0.0077 per minute**, which is the November 2026 pay-as-you-go price for Nova-3 monolingual **streaming**. The server posts a finished WAV to the pre-recorded `/v1/listen` endpoint, whose list price for the same model is **$0.0043 per minute**, billed per second, with smart formatting included and filler words unpriced ([Deepgram pricing](https://deepgram.com/pricing)). The AssemblyAI constant **$0.0025 per minute** still equals Universal-2 at **$0.15 per hour**. Universal-3.5 Pro is **$0.21 per hour**, and the prompting add-on that one of AssemblyAI’s own pages says is how that model keeps fillers is another **$0.05 per hour** ([AssemblyAI pricing](https://www.assemblyai.com/pricing)). Word start and end times, which the 300 ms pause rule depends on, come back from Deepgram, Speechmatics, AssemblyAI’s sync API, Groq, and Gemini file transcription. None of those docs publish a timestamp error in milliseconds, so “good enough for 300 ms” is a resolution claim, not an accuracy claim.

Sentiment add-ons do not hear inflection. Deepgram Audio Intelligence and AssemblyAI sentiment (**+$0.02 per hour**) run on the transcript ([Deepgram pricing](https://deepgram.com/pricing); [AssemblyAI pricing](https://www.assemblyai.com/pricing)). Hume’s Expression Measurement API does score the audio, including a documented “monotone” voice attribute among 190 voice descriptors, and it bills the full file with a 10-second minimum ([Hume overview](https://dev.hume.ai/expression-measurement/docs/overview.mdx); [Hume billing](https://dev.hume.ai/expression-measurement/docs/billing)). The official pricing page fetched on 8 November 2026 no longer lists a per-minute rate. Two secondary 2026 sources print **$0.0639 per minute** of audio, which would be most of the bill and still does not document an uptalk slope or a raw energy contour ([Jeff Turner voice-AI cost PDF](https://www.jeffturner.info/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/voice-ai-cost-breakdown.pdf)). Commercial coaching apps advertise the same surface the code already has. Yoodli reports pacing, fillers, conciseness, and structure; Orai reports fillers, pace, clarity, and energy; none of the pages fetched name Praat, a pitch model, or a speech-to-text vendor ([Yoodli](https://yoodli.ai/use-cases/public-speaking); [Orai](https://orai.com/product/)). Poised shut down on 9 October 2026 ([app.poised.com](https://app.poised.com/)).

Haiku’s live price was not on Anthropic’s page in this research. The only retrieved token rate is a deprecated AssemblyAI gateway card for Claude 4.5 Haiku at **$1 / $5 per million** input and output tokens. The repo logs **$0.10 / $0.50**. At a stated guess of 2,000 input and 400 output tokens, that is **$0.0004** on the code’s constants and **$0.004** on the gateway card. Praat is local and costs nothing marginal.

## The quality upgrade at this budget is a Praat setting

Toastmasters, the public rubric with the clearest fetched dimensions, grades vocal variety as the joint use of **tone, speed, and volume**, and grades articulation, pausing, and voice quality (nasal, breathy, harsh) as separate judgments ([Toastmasters 8104E1](https://content.toastmasters.org/image/upload/v1741989017/8104E1-evaluation-resource-ff.pdf); [Toastmasters 8104E2](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/department-documents/education-documents/evaluation-resources/english/8104e2-speech-profile-ff.pdf)). Rate, pause counts, filler counts, and a speaker-normalized pitch spread have a direct acoustic definition. Whether the variety landed on the right words, whether the voice was shrill or full, and whether the answer had a point are coach judgments. Vojo already computes the countable subset and leaves volume on the floor: RMS energy is calculated and withheld from both the score and the model, and raw RMS moves with mic distance and automatic gain. The open feature set built for this job, eGeMAPS, uses auditory loudness, harmonics-to-noise ratio, H1–H2, formant summaries, and voiced versus unvoiced region lengths. On acted emotion corpora its 88 parameters matched a much larger brute-force set for binary arousal (average UAR about **79.7** versus **78.0**) ([Eyben et al. 2016](https://lab-msp.com/MSP/publications/Eyben_2016.pdf)). Those are emotion-classification numbers, not agreement with a speaking coach, and they are the right family of features for the voice-quality words the cards do not yet have. openSMILE extracts them on CPU. For a one-minute clip that cost is noise next to transcription.

The pitch tracker itself is a serious estimator with documented failure modes that land on this product’s gates. Praat’s raw autocorrelation keeps on the order of 15 candidates per 10 ms frame and tracks a path through them, with an octave cost precisely because undertones of F0 are equally strong even in a clean periodic signal ([Praat pitch analysis](https://praat.org/manual/pitch_analysis_by_raw_autocorrelation.html)). Boersma’s 1993 evaluation found local octave errors around **10%** when amplitude modulation was 5% and F0 sat near the floor; the path finder is described as removing isolated ones ([Boersma 1993](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/archive/1993/1993-ifaproc17-Boersma.pdf)). An octave error is **12 semitones**. Median-normalization does not absorb it, and it dwarfs the **1.5 semitone** monotone gate. Creak can fall to about **30 Hz**, under the 75 Hz floor, so fry either vanishes from the spread or enters as an octave jump ([Praat Intro 4.2](https://praat.org/manual/Intro_4_2__Configuring_the_pitch_contour.html)). Since 2023 Praat’s filtered autocorrelation, a Gaussian low-pass before the correlation, is the method its own manual recommends when a 75 Hz floor meets noise or when a formant is an integer multiple of F0 ([how to choose a pitch analysis method](https://praat.org/manual/how_to_choose_a_pitch_analysis_method.html)). Vojo’s settings match the raw method, and the ceiling is **500 Hz** where Praat’s current raw-autocorrelation standard is **600 Hz**. Switching to filtered autocorrelation is a configuration change in the library already imported. It is not a published error rate on 60-second smartphone monologues; no such study was found, and the 1.5 / 2.5 semitone gates and the 4 semitones-per-second uptalk slope are unvalidated against listeners.

Neural pitch is not the first purchase. PENN’s FCNF0++, trained on speech and music, reached raw pitch accuracy **0.9825** and **12.45 cents** error on laryngograph-labeled read speech plus synthetic music, against pYIN at **0.8477**, and it ran at about **11× realtime** on one Intel i9 core ([Morrison et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.12258)). CREPE’s headline numbers are on synthetic music, its authors flag mobile-phone speech as motivation and do not report it, and a small real-speech comparison found its voicing errors at about **7%** and **19%** depending on direction ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.06182v1); [Joglekar and Sepúlveda](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.14357)). emotion2vec improves categorical emotion accuracy into the low 70s weighted accuracy on IEMOCAP and does not emit a monotone score, an uptalk slope, or a filler tag ([Ma et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.931.pdf)).

Pronunciation APIs answer a different question, and they cost more. Azure, Speechace, and ELSA all accept unscripted audio. Azure’s accuracy score is how closely phones match a native-speaker model; prosody (stress, intonation, rhythm) is an en-US add-on, and Microsoft warns that the unscripted recognizer is a different, less trusted transcript than ordinary speech-to-text ([Microsoft pronunciation assessment](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/how-to-pronunciation-assessment)). Speechace Premium, the spontaneous tier, is **$0.0125 per 15 seconds**, so **$0.05** for a 60-second answer, and it returns exam-style coherence and task scores more than consonant crispness ([Speechace plans](https://www.speechace.com/api-plans/)). ELSA’s unscripted standard rate on its own page is **$0.02 per 15 seconds**, so **$0.08** ([ELSA API](https://elsaspeak.com/en/elsa-api/)). A clear non-General-American answer can look “inaccurate,” and a mumbled answer whose phones still match the model can look fine. That is accent coaching. It is a weak proxy for the mumbling, tight jaw, and lazy consonants in the Toastmasters voice profile. Gemini live audio input is published at about **$0.005 per minute** plus text output, cheap enough to draft a comment from measurements the code already trusts, and it is the wrong system of record for a number a user might dispute: the same wav through Praat yields the same semitone spread, and no retrieved study calibrates an audio model’s “monotone: 62” against that spread or against coaches ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)).

## Coach labels top out near human agreement

A model trained to imitate coaches is capped by how much coaches agree, and on the traits Vojo counts they agree less cleanly than the measurement does. A 2025 meta-analysis of automated speech evaluation (67 studies, 392 effects) found overall human–machine **r = 0.654**: delivery **0.784**, fluency **0.618**, pronunciation **0.606**, content **0.574**, grammar and vocabulary **0.499** ([Language Testing](https://doi.org/10.1177/02655322251387816)). SpeechRater, the long-running ETS delivery-and-language regressor, reached speaker-level **r = 0.61** against human inter-rater **r = 0.63** on the same TOEFL Practice Online responses ([Handbook of Test Development chapter](https://ebrary.net/76697/education/reliability_validity)). In operation, one human plus SpeechRater reaches assessment reliability **0.83**, against **0.76** for the machine alone and **0.75** for humans alone ([ETS TOEFL Research Insight](https://www.in.ets.org/content/dam/ets-india/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-insight-s1v3.pdf)). The production design at the lab that has done this longest is the hybrid Vojo already resembles: measured features plus a separate judgment, not one network that swallows the waveform. On public speaking specifically, five student raters told to ignore content and pronunciation still only reached pairwise Spearman **ρ = 0.47** on 684 thirty-second clips, and a speaker-independent acoustic regressor reached **ρ = 0.60** ([Azaïs et al. 2015](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2015/azais15_interspeech.pdf)). An ETS public-speaking prototype found expert–nonexpert reliability of **0.75** for vocal expression and **0.63** holistic, and **0.41** for persuasion and **0.23** for word choice ([ETS Research Report](https://doi.org/10.1002/ets2.12083)).

Phone-level articulation is a different label, and it is not a vibe score. On SpeechOcean762’s read-aloud set, Microsoft’s research system reached Pearson **0.656** against a human-parity figure of **0.679** ([Microsoft pronunciation blog](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azure-ai-services-blog/speech-service-update-%E2%80%93-hierarchical-transformer-for-pronunciation-assessment/3740866)). Expert annotators agreed only moderately on where the error was: **kappa 0.492** ([Loukina et al. 2015](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2015/loukina15b_interspeech.pdf)). Three pronunciation experts who had just been standardized against each other reached ICC **0.882**; a generative scorer against their average managed **r = 0.271** on individual phonemes, and its scores were higher than the experts on every sub-element ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0354603)). Fluent coaching prose and a correct count are different skills. Google’s audio supervised-tuning format is audio in, text out. It teaches a target string. It does not check filler counts or pitch ([Gemini audio tuning, updated 7 November 2026](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/tuning/audio)). OpenAI’s current realtime audio models list fine-tuning as unsupported ([GPT-Realtime-2](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-realtime-2)).

The data behind the published numbers is smaller, and more domain-specific, than “train on coaches until it is incredibly accurate.” Disfluency taggers use on the order of **121 hours** of already-annotated Switchboard. A BERT tagger hit weighted F1 **0.85** on those manual transcripts and **0.76** zero-shot on stuttered speech; an audio model with no transcript managed **0.68** and **0.48** ([FluencyBank Timestamped](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12379651/)). A Switchboard-trained detector scored F1 **39.7** on Supreme Court transcripts ([Zayats et al.](https://aclanthology.org/N15-1161.pdf)). Scoring models are often fit on about **1,200** short exam responses. None of that is coach-rated 60-second phone monologues. US speech-language pathologists earned a mean **$47.20** an hour in May 2025 ([BLS OEWS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ocwage.t01.htm)). A loaded coach above that wage, a few minutes per clip, and a second rater, which the reliability literature requires, puts a 200-to-500-clip calibration set in the thousands of dollars and a training set with written notes from two professionals well above that. GPU time is the small line: late-2026 on-demand A100 80 GB rates sit around **$1.50 to $1.59** per hour ([OpenGPU Radar](https://opengpuradar.com/workloads/llm-fine-tuning)). An always-on GPU to serve a 7B audio model is on the order of a thousand dollars a month before anyone uses it. One minute of Universal-2 is **$0.0025** and one minute of Nova-3 pre-recorded is **$0.0043**, with no model to host.

Accent and channel will move a learned articulation score for reasons that are not skill. A 2025 review treats recording device and audio quality as construct-irrelevant bias, and Duolingo’s intelligibility model still reported lower scores on Windows audio and disparities by language family ([Cai et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/lang.70000); [Duolingo English Test](https://blog.englishtest.duolingo.com/new-research-in-language-learning-a-pronunciation-scoring-model-built-around-intelligibility-not-imitation/)). Listeners’ comprehension of TOEFL responses tracked human scores at **0.97** and **0.81**, and tracked SpeechRater at **0.55** and **0.37** ([Handbook chapter](https://ebrary.net/76697/education/reliability_validity)). A delivery model can match raters and still miss whether anyone understood the answer. In the interview-dialog study, more than **400** of **1,893** utterances were unscorable because the audio was poor ([Ramanarayanan et al. 2017](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2017/ramanarayanan17b_interspeech.pdf)). Practice recordings are personal data. Illinois BIPA names a voiceprint, and complaints filed in May 2026 are testing whether training embeddings from public recordings count; those are allegations, not holdings ([ABA BIPA note](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/class-actions-derivative-suits/voiceprints-ai-bipa-new-trends-biometric-privacy-litigation/); [Code on Trial](https://www.codeontrial.ai/p/voiceprints-under-bipa)). Consent to grade a session is not consent to fine-tune on it.

## Conclusion

The next dollar of quality is a calibration set, not a new network. Two coaches double-scoring a few hundred real Vojo clips, stratified by accent and phone, will show whether the **1 per minute** filler free pass, the **130–170** WPM band, the **1.5 semitone** monotone flag, and the **4 semitones per second** uptalk slope rank answers the way those coaches do. If the coaches disagree with each other more than they disagree with the current grader, there is no accuracy left to buy. The same labels should move thresholds. They should not become a training loss for counts the transcript and Praat already produce. A small ranker on the features the code already computes, or on a frozen speech embedding, is worth attempting only after that eval shows the hybrid mis-orders delivery, and the honest expectation is correlation in the 0.6s, the SpeechRater neighborhood, on held-out speakers.

Until that set exists, three changes are supported by the sources and by the code path as it stands. Log and, if the invoice confirms it, pay the pre-recorded Nova-3 rate for the `/v1/listen` call already in `deepgram.py`, and keep Universal-2 until a real clip proves whether Universal-3.5 Pro’s boolean still emits “um” or only a prompt does. Point Parselmouth at filtered autocorrelation and start scoring auditory loudness, harmonics-to-noise, and the pause and drift fields the pipeline already calculates, so volume and a slow pitch climb can affect the grade they are currently invisible to. Keep every number in the coaching sentence copied from those measurements. The generative-scoring result says a model that sounds like a coach will otherwise report a filler count the transcript does not contain, and the user will believe the sentence.
